# The original object and the exact range

The row is the real polynomial formed from ordered lists of disjoint nonempty blocks covering the labelled ground set, giving one marking factor to each block of size at least j. In the formal definition, a surjection onto an ordered finite set specifies the blocks in their listed order. RootArray records a strictly increasing list of negative roots accounting for the full degree with multiplicities.

For each fixed real C, the source proves existence of a natural K(C) such that j≥K(C), j≤n≤Cj and 1≤h≤j imply full strictly negative-root interlacing of row(n,j) and row(n+h,j). When the upper degree is equal, the old root is leftmost and the new rightmost; at a rise the new row occupies both ends.

The first-row polynomial at an integral limiting ratio may lose one degree in the hard-rod limit. The same holds for its full-period successor. In the finite rows the two large negative roots are separated using Vieta's identity and a bound on the ratio of the two original normalized leading coefficients. This includes the initial chamber where the limiting old row is constant. Degree-preserving limits, the two disappearing roots, positive affine transport, and the compactness step are contained in HardRodFullPeriodBranches, HardRodFullPeriodRows and HardRodWindowVerified. HardRodPeriodOrder proves that the adjacent and full-period checks suffice for the intermediate shifts.

The theorem is a bounded-ratio result. The independent paper theorem for j and n/j both tending to infinity is outside this formal closure.
